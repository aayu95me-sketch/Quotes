What each platform actually costs (the honest version)

Topmate: simplest to start, most expensive to stay

Topmate charges no subscription, just a 10% platform fee plus a 2% transaction fee, with 18% GST on the fee, so 14.16% on a rupee sale through your own link (Topmate's own example: ₹141.60 on ₹1,000). International payments cost more once currency conversion and cross-border fees apply. It is a clean product and a fine place to launch. But if you sell at any real volume, that cut becomes the single biggest line item eating your earnings. If you are already feeling it, it is worth comparing 
Topmate alternatives in India
.

Who it is for:
 creators testing the waters who value simplicity over margin.

SuperProfile: 499 rupees a month plus 5%

SuperProfile is a genuine India-first player with a large creator base, but for Indian sellers it costs more than a flat 5% at every sales level. Its free Starter plan cannot sell at all, so selling means the Creator plan at 99 rupees for the first month and 499 rupees a month after that. On top of the subscription, 
SuperProfile's own help centre
 lists a platform fee of 5% + GST on every sale for Indian accounts, which is 5.9% of each sale. The 0% commission on its pricing page applies only to Global accounts outside India. On 1,00,000 rupees of monthly sales that is 6,399 rupees on SuperProfile against 5,000 rupees on a flat 5%.

Who it is for:
 established creators doing enough volume that a paid tier's lower rate beats the free tier's 10%.

Instamojo: fine for payments, built for stores not creators

Instamojo has no setup or maintenance fee and charges 2% + 3 rupees on regular payments, but 5% + 3 rupees on digital goods, with 18% GST added on the fee. It is a solid payment and store tool. It is not built around the creator monetization flow (1:1 sessions, webinars, cohorts) the way a creator-first platform is, so you end up stitching tools together.

Who it is for:
 sellers who mainly need a checkout and store, not a creator toolkit.

Graphy: powerful LMS, punishing India pricing

Graphy (formerly Spayee) is a capable course platform, but its India pricing is harsh: monthly plans from 1,999 to 8,400 rupees, a 10% revenue share plus GST on every transaction, and a one-time 19,999 rupee onboarding fee. You pay to join, pay every month, and still give up 10% of every sale. The international USD plans charge 0% transaction fees, which tells you exactly who the India model is designed to squeeze.

Who it is for:
 larger course businesses that need a full LMS and can absorb the fixed costs.

Peerseek: flat 5 percent, nothing hidden

Peerseek charges a 0% platform fee and a flat 5% transaction fee. No subscription. No onboarding charge. No free tier that quietly takes 10%. As with every platform, standard payment-gateway charges can apply on top, but there is no extra platform cut layered over it. You accept UPI, cards, netbanking, wallets, and international payments, and you sell 1:1 sessions, 
host a paid webinar in India
, run cohorts, and sell digital products from one place. On a 1,00,000 rupee month, that is roughly the difference between keeping 95,000 and keeping 85,840 on Topmate.

Who it is for:
 creators who actually sell and want the most of every rupee to reach their own bank account.

The fees nobody advertises

GST on the fee (18%):
 most platforms add it on top of their cut. A 5% fee is effectively about 5.9% after GST.

Payment-gateway charges:
 these can sit on top of any platform's fee, including the lowest ones. Compare all-in.

International payment surcharges:
 cross-border and currency conversion add to a platform's fees on top of the domestic rate.

Onboarding and setup fees:
 Graphy's 19,999 rupees is the loud example. Always check for a one-time charge.

Payout timelines:
 a low fee means less if your money is locked for T+7. Look for T+1 to T+2 settlement.

Free plan fine print:
 free almost always means a higher percentage cut. Read what the free tier actually takes.

How to choose (a 30 second rule)

Selling occasionally, low volume?
 Simplicity wins. A no-subscription platform with a flat fee beats paying monthly.

Selling seriously every month?
 Do the math on total cost, not the headline rate. A cheaper tier with a 12,000 rupee yearly plan only pays off above a certain volume.

Selling 1:1 calls, webinars, cohorts, and products together?
 Pick a creator-first platform that does all of it in one place, so you are not stitching a store, a scheduler, and a checkout together.

Taking international payments?
 Check the cross-border rate specifically. Domestic and international fees are often very different.
