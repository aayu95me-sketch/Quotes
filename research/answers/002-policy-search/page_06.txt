Instagram AI Labels: Rules, Removal and Correct Use (2026)

Instagram AI label rules for 2026: what must be labeled, the AI-generated profile label, how to add or remove labels, and how to appeal a wrong one.

Quick answer

Instagram requires an AI label on photorealistic video and realistic-sounding audio made or altered with AI; images are exempt but can be labeled automatically when the file carries AI signals. Since August 31, 2026, profiles that feature an AI-generated person should turn on the AI-generated profile label: Instagram says labeled profiles keep their reach, while unlabeled ones it detects can stop being recommended until they add it or win an appeal in Account Status. You can turn the profile label off and report wrong post labels, but stripping signals to dodge a required label risks reach and account penalties. Sponsored posts still need the paid partnership label.

Instagram has two AI labels: one on posts, required for realistic AI video and audio, and one on the profile, expected of any account that features an AI-generated person.
 Images don't need a label, but Instagram may add one anyway when a file carries AI signals. Labeling correctly costs nothing in reach by Instagram's own account; hiding an AI character is what gets a profile pulled from recommendations.

Checked October 2026 against Instagram's Help Center, Recommendation Guidelines and @creators blog, Meta's Transparency Center, the Oversight Board and the FTC. Platform rules change often, so check the linked pages before a campaign.

What the AI label on Instagram means: the two labels

Most confusion about "the AI label" comes from mixing up two different things. One sits on individual posts, reels and stories and says AI was involved in making that content. The other sits on the account and says the person you are looking at is not a real human. They have different rules, different switches and different consequences.

The rule: what you must label

Instagram's 
Help Center
 sets it out plainly: "Meta requires you to label content you share that has photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that has been digitally generated or altered, including with AI." The label goes on before you share. Instagram's own examples of content that needs it include a realistic video of people walking around an outdoor market, an audio clip of two people talking, a song with AI-generated vocals, and a reel narrated with a realistic AI voiceover.

Two examples that don't: a cartoon-style landscape video, and any image, because "Meta does not require you to label images that have been created or altered with AI." The word "altered" matters. By that wording, a real video with an AI-cloned voice or an AI face swap needs the label, even if most of the footage is genuine. Meta doesn't set a threshold for small fixes such as color correction or noise reduction. Our rule of thumb: if an edit changes what a realistic video shows or what realistic audio says, label it.

For an AI influencer, that table has a practical consequence. A feed of still images of your character needs no post-level label at all, though many will pick one up automatically. The moment you post a realistic talking video or a voice note, the requirement applies to every one of those posts.

What happens if you skip a required label

Meta's 
Misinformation policy
 says it requires people to use the AI disclosure tool for this content "and we may apply penalties if they fail to do so." In its 
March 2026 decision
 on an AI-generated war video, the Oversight Board reported Meta's explanation that these penalties are applied on escalation in response to repeated failures, and may affect content distribution or temporarily suspend certain account features. In a 
May 2026 response
, Meta said it is exploring ways to apply them at scale. Today enforcement is patchy; the direction of travel is not.

How a label ends up on your post

A label can come from you or from Meta. Meta's side works from signals in the file, not from looking at the picture and judging whether it seems fake:

From export to label

01

You export a file

Your editor or generator may write C2PA Content Credentials or IPTC AI tags into it.

02

You upload it

Optionally switching on Add AI label yourself.

03

Meta reads the signals

Meta AI output, Content Credentials and AI origin tags are detected.

04

Label is shown

On the post if AI-created; in the menu if only edited; a more prominent label for high-risk content.

Meta AI tools.
 Content created or edited with Meta AI may be labeled automatically, including when you download it and upload it again.

Industry-standard signals.
 Files with signals that they were created with AI, including from other companies' tools, are labeled. In May 2026 Meta said its systems read Content Credentials (the C2PA standard) and the AI origin tags that tools embed in image files.

Edited, not created.
 When the signals say content was only modified with AI, the label appears in the post's Options menu rather than on the post.

High-risk content.
 Meta may add a more prominent label to altered content that risks misleading people on a matter of public importance, after human review.

Detection has gaps in both directions. The Oversight Board's March 2026 decision said Meta could automatically label only static images, so realistic video and audio depend on people disclosing. Instagram itself warns that not all AI content contains the information needed to identify it. And it misfires: on September 4, 2026, 
The Verge reported
 AI labels on photos edited with assistive tools such as Canva's Background Remover, while fully generated images from other tools went unlabeled in its tests. A label isn't proof that content is AI, and a missing label isn't proof that it's real.

The AI-generated profile label (formerly AI creator)

Instagram started an optional "AI creator" account label in May 2026 for creators who regularly used AI tools, 
Social Media Today reported
. On 
August 31, 2026
 it renamed it "AI-generated profile" and narrowed it, saying people don't like seeing a profile that seems human, only to find out later that the person featured is AI-generated.

No change

Instagram's stated reach effect for profiles that add the AI-generated profile label themselves

Help Center: the label "will neither help nor hurt your account's reach"

Unlabeled profiles Instagram detects can become ineligible for recommendations

Creators who only use AI tools in their process do not need the label

Source: 
Instagram Help Center and @creators blog, August 31, 2026

Who needs it:
 profiles that feature an AI-generated person rather than a human. People who use AI to edit photos, polish captions or make graphics don't.

The written rule:
 Instagram's 
Recommendation Guidelines
 say it tries not to recommend accounts that repeatedly and recently shared content prominently featuring a photorealistic AI-generated person, unless the account has added the AI-generated profile label.

What that means in practice:
 recommendation is how posts reach non-followers in Reels and Explore. An unlabeled AI persona that gets detected keeps its followers but loses the discovery surfaces new accounts depend on.

Where it shows:
 on the profile, in search, and next to your content and comments. On a post that already has its own AI label, the post label takes priority.

How to add an AI label on Instagram

Adding labels in the Instagram app

1

Profile: Edit profile

Go to your profile, tap Edit profile and turn on AI-generated profile. Do this first for an AI persona.

2

Post or reel: before sharing

Create the post, then on the final screen tap the toggle next to Add AI label.

3

Story: Options menu

Share a photo or video to your story, tap Options, then Label AI, then turn on Add AI label.

4

Ads and branded posts

Add the paid partnership label as well; the AI label does not cover ad disclosure.

Use the mobile app: Instagram's Help Center says the labeling feature isn't available on computers. If you schedule posts from a desktop tool, check whether it passes an AI label through, and if not, publish realistic video from the app. If you don't label something yourself, Instagram notes a label may still be added when Meta detects AI.

You can also label content that doesn't strictly need it, such as AI images of your character. Nothing in Instagram's documentation says a voluntary label lowers reach, and it keeps your feed consistent with your profile label. Whether to do it is a brand choice, not a rule.

How to remove an AI label on Instagram

"How to remove the AI label" is one of the most searched questions on this topic, and the honest answer depends on which label and why it is there.

The profile label:
 switch off AI-generated profile in Edit profile. Do this only if your account no longer features an AI-generated person, for example if you used the old AI creator label because you edit with AI tools. Instagram invited those creators to review the renamed label and remove it if it no longer fits.

A label you added to a post:
 the Help Center explains adding the label before sharing, not removing it afterwards. Check the toggle before you tap Share.

A label Instagram added:
 there is no published switch to remove an automatic label. Because it comes from signals in the file, the fix is upstream: find out which app in your workflow writes AI metadata into its exports, and test a few exports before using that app for brand work.

Why gaming the label backfires

Searches for "how to avoid the AI label" usually lead to advice about stripping metadata, screenshotting files or re-encoding video. Here is why that is a bad trade for an AI influencer account:

The obligation doesn't depend on detection.
 Realistic AI video and audio must be labeled whether or not Meta can see the signals. Removing them only removes the evidence that you skipped a required label.

The profile rule looks at the account, not the file.
 Recommendation limits apply to accounts that keep featuring a photorealistic AI person without the profile label. Clean metadata doesn't change what is in the pictures.

The penalty is on reach, which is the thing you wanted to protect.
 Instagram says a labeled profile keeps its reach. An unlabeled one that gets detected can lose recommendations until it adds the label or wins an appeal.

Brands check.
 A partner who discovers an undisclosed AI persona after a campaign has a legitimate grievance, and that costs more than any label.

The legitimate goal behind most removal searches is a real photo that got mislabeled because of an editing tool. That is fixable: change how you export, and report the wrong label.

If Instagram gets it wrong: the appeal path

Your profile was flagged as AI-generated but features a real person.
 Instagram notifies detected profiles. Open 
Account Status
 in settings, check for limits on recommendations and appeal. Instagram says a successful appeal makes the profile and its content eligible for recommendations again.

Some posts can't be recommended.
 Professional accounts can see in Account Status whether content is ineligible, with sample posts, and 
may be able to request a review
.

A post carries an AI label it shouldn't.
 Tap the label or the post's menu to see why. The Help Center describes no appeal for post labels, so use 
Report a problem
 with a screenshot and a short note on how the content was made.

Keep your originals.
 Raw files and a note of each edit make it easy to explain a disputed label to Instagram, a client or a brand.

What this means for AI influencer accounts

If you run a virtual character, the rules point one way: disclose at the account level from day one, label realistic video and voice on every post, and treat automatic labels on images as normal. Instagram does not say the profile label satisfies the separate requirement for realistic video and audio, so do both. Keep the content original too; our guide to

Sponsored posts: Instagram's rules and the FTC

AI labels and money disclosures are separate obligations. Instagram's 
branded content rules
 require the paid partnership label "when there's an exchange of value," including free products. In the US, the FTC's 2023 update to the Endorsement Guides 
clarified
 that endorsements can include virtual influencers, and warned that a platform's built-in disclosure tool might not be adequate on its own. The FTC's 
Disclosures 101
 says to put the disclosure with the endorsement in plain words such as "ad" and, for video, in the video itself. A disclosure that lives only on the profile page is not enough for paid posts.

The FTC also says you can't describe experience with a product you haven't used. Our reading for virtual characters: don't script your AI persona to claim personal results it cannot have. Other countries have their own advertising rules, so check where your audience and partners are.

Instagram AI labels: FAQ

Do I have to label AI content on Instagram?

Only some of it. Meta requires a label on photorealistic video and realistic-sounding audio that was digitally generated or altered, including with AI, such as a reel narrated with a realistic AI voiceover. Images do not have to be labeled, and neither does cartoon-style video, but Instagram may label any content automatically when it detects AI signals in the file.

What does the AI label on Instagram mean?

A label on a post (now called "AI content", earlier "AI info" and "Made with AI") means the post was created or edited with AI, either because the creator said so or because Meta detected AI signals. The AI-generated profile label on an account means the profile regularly features an AI-generated person rather than a human. Neither label is a penalty.

How do I remove an AI label on Instagram?

You can switch the AI-generated profile label off in Edit profile if your account no longer features an AI-generated person. For posts, Instagram documents adding the label before you share but not removing it afterwards, and it publishes no way to remove a label it added automatically. If a label is wrong, report it with a description of how the content was made. Never remove or skip a label on content that requires one.

Does an AI label reduce reach on Instagram?

Instagram says the AI-generated profile label neither helps nor hurts reach, and that creators who add it proactively will not see a change. Meta has not said that a post-level AI label lowers reach. The reach penalties it describes are for skipping labels: profiles that feature an AI-generated person without the profile label can stop being recommended to non-followers.

Why did Instagram label my photo as AI?

Instagram labels content automatically when it was made with Meta AI tools or when the file carries industry-standard signals, such as C2PA Content Credentials or IPTC metadata, saying AI was used. In September 2026, The Verge reported labels on photos edited with assistive tools such as Canva's Background Remover. Check what your editing apps write into the files you export.

Can I appeal an AI label or reduced reach on Instagram?

Yes for the profile: if Instagram flags your account as an AI-generated profile and limits recommendations, you can appeal in Account Status, and a successful appeal makes the profile eligible for recommendations again. For a label on a single post, the Help Center describes no formal appeal; use Report a problem with a screenshot and an explanation.

Do AI influencers have to disclose sponsored posts?

Yes. Instagram requires the paid partnership label whenever there is an exchange of value with a brand, and in the US the FTC says endorsements can include virtual influencers. A disclosure that appears only in the bio is not enough for paid posts, and the AI label does not replace an ad disclosure.
