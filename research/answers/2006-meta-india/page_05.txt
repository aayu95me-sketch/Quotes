4. Facebook Monetization Eligibility Requirements (2026)

Before Facebook even thinks about paying you, every creator has to clear the same baseline. No shortcuts. No hacks. This is your entry pass. Miss one thing here, and Facebook monetization stays locked.

Think of this as the moment where Facebook asks, are you creating seriously or just posting casually?

Professional Architecture Comes First

You cannot monetize a private profile. Period.

To avoid 
Facebook monetization requirements
, you must either run a proper Facebook Page or switch on Professional Mode. This tells Facebook you are open to monetization and ready to follow platform rules. Without this setup, no monetization tools will even appear, no matter how good your content is.

If you are trying to figure out how to monetize Facebook page content, this is step one. No professional setup, no payouts.

Partner Monetization Policies Still Apply

Facebook monetization is not global by default. You must be in a supported country and be at least 18 years old to qualify. These rules come straight from the Facebook monetization policy, and they apply to everyone.

There is no workaround here. If your location or age does not meet the criteria, Facebook monetization eligibility stops before it starts.

Original Content Is Non-Negotiable

This is where most creators fail.

Reposting clips from TikTok or other platforms without meaningful changes is the fastest way to get flagged. Watermarks, recycled edits, or low-effort reposts trigger Meta’s AI instantly. Once your Page is marked for unoriginal content, monetization gets paused or blocked.

Facebook content guidelines are clear on this. If you want access to Facebook monetization, your content must show original effort. Your voice, your edits, your perspective.

Meet these baseline rules, and you are officially in the game. Skip them, and no amount of views will unlock earnings.

6. Common Pitfalls: Why Applications Get Rejected

Common Pitfalls: Why Your Facebook Monetization Applications Get Rejected

Hitting follower milestones does not guarantee approval. Many creators meet the numbers and still fail Facebook monetization requirements because of small but costly mistakes.

Here are the most common ones:

The 90-Day Rule

Your Page must be at least three months old. New Pages often fail Facebook monetization eligibility because Meta needs time to verify you are a real creator, not a bot or spam account.

Static Video Violations

Uploading podcast audio with a still image no longer qualifies. This format fails Facebook video monetization eligibility because it lacks original visual content.

Copyrighted Music Issues

Using music outside the Meta Sound Collection can instantly block monetization. Even one mistake here can pause earnings under Facebook monetization policy.

Avoid these, and you save yourself months of frustration and reapplications.

FAQs

What are Facebook’s monetization eligibility requirements in 2026?

You need Professional Mode or a Page, to be 18+, based in a supported country, and your Page must be at least 90 days old.

What content criteria does Facebook check before approving monetization?

Original content only. Reposted clips, static-image-plus-audio uploads, and unlicensed music all block eligibility.

How do I monetize my Facebook Page in 2026?

Switch to Professional Mode, meet the eligibility and content criteria above, then hit the follower and watch-time benchmark for the specific tool you want, whether that’s Stars, Subscriptions, or Unified Content Ads.

Which Facebook monetization tool should I start with?

Most creators start with Unified Content Ads or Facebook Stars since they have the lowest entry bar (5,000 and 500 followers respectively). Add Fan Subscriptions or Brand Collaborations once your audience and engagement grow.

What happens if my Facebook monetization application gets rejected?

You can reapply once you’ve fixed the issue, usually after your next Meta Business Suite eligibility check. Common fixes include removing static-image-plus-audio posts, swapping in licensed music, or waiting out the 90-day Page age requirement.
