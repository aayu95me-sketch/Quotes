Facebook Content Monetization in 2026: What Creators Need to Know

 by 

Elnathan Chiteve

 | 
Oct 16, 2025
 | 
Content Creation
, 
Social Media

Updated 26 September 2026.
 Facebook monetisation has changed since this article was first published. This guide explains what Meta currently says about its Content Monetization programme and what creators can check in their own accounts.

Facebook can be a meaningful part of a creator business, but earning is not automatic. Access, eligible formats, policies and payouts depend on the programme and your account. Start with the tools shown in your Professional Dashboard rather than a follower-count rule from an old blog post.

How Facebook Content Monetization works in 2026

Meta says Facebook Content Monetization can pay eligible creators for content performance across Reels, longer videos, Stories, photos and text posts. The programme is 
invite only
. Creators can express interest in the Facebook app through Professional Dashboard → Monetization → Content Monetization, and should follow the current setup and eligibility information shown there. 
Meta’s March 2026 update
 explains the programme and newer metrics.

There is no reliable universal promise that a particular number of followers or views will get an invitation. Availability and requirements can change, so use the account’s dashboard and 
Meta’s Content Monetization help page
 as the source for your own eligibility and payout setup.

Original content matters

Meta says it is prioritising original work and reducing distribution of unoriginal content. Filming or producing your own material is a strong starting point. If you use third-party clips, add meaningful new information, analysis or creative transformation. Minor edits, reposts and reaction-only clips may be treated as unoriginal. Read 
Meta’s originality guidance
 and its 
Content Monetization Policies
 before relying on any format.

A practical plan for creators

Check your dashboard.
 Open Professional Dashboard on your Page or professional profile and review the Monetization section. Look for available tools, invitations and any policy issues.

Build around your own perspective.
 Share original videos, useful explanations, stories, photos or text that your audience has a reason to engage with.

Review performance, not just views.
 Meta has introduced metrics such as qualified views, earnings rate and non-qualified views. Use the information available in your account to learn what qualifies and resonates.

Keep a wider income plan.
 Brand partnerships, services, products or subscriptions may be relevant alongside platform payouts. Do not plan a fixed income from views you cannot control.

Questions creators often ask

Is Facebook Content Monetization open to everyone?

No. Meta describes it as invite only. You can check your account’s tools and, where available, express interest through the Professional Dashboard.

Do Stories and text posts count?

Meta says eligible Stories, photo and text posts can be included alongside video. Eligibility still depends on your programme access and applicable policies.

How much will I earn?

There is no fixed rate or guaranteed return. Performance and qualified views affect earnings, and the dashboard is the place to review your actual account data.
